How to read this / how it was made

Policy vs. value disagreement

Every legal move carries its policy probability and the child value — the value head's verdict on the position after that move, from the mover's side. A move the policy loves but the value dislikes (or vice versa) is where search and network fight each other.

Where the answer forms

The value and policy are decoded from every layer's residual stream through the final norm and the real heads (a logit lens). If the top move is already locked in by layer 6 and nothing moves after, the last ten layers are not doing policy work.

Occlusion saliency

Each non-king piece is deleted in turn and the position re-evaluated. The square shows the swing in value, or in the probability of the move the model wanted. Big swings mark the pieces the verdict actually rests on.

Attention and rollout

Attention is recomputed from each layer's captured input using the model's own projections and its chess-relation bias, then checked against the real forward pass. Rollout multiplies the head-averaged, residual-corrected maps through the stack and reads the game-state token rows.
